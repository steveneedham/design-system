01 / Scope and method

What was examined.

The review covered the portfolio homepage, Accessibility & accommodations page, Urban Strategist consulting page, asset and component specimens, and the Technology Ecosystem and How I Think case studies.

  • Source checks for page language, titles, headings, landmarks, alternative text, form titles, link purpose, reduced-motion rules, focus treatment, and duplicate identifiers.
  • Rendered-page checks at desktop and narrow viewports, including responsive reflow, visual hierarchy, and visible feedback routes.
  • Manual source review of focus treatment, color-dependent meaning, motion, embedded content, image descriptions, and accessible alternatives.
02 / Findings and actions

Release findings.

Material findings addressed for this release

  1. Accessibility had no release gate. Added the eight-part acceptance layer and required evidence in the definition of done.
  2. Feedback and accommodations were difficult to find. Added a dedicated public page, footer feedback route, alternative-format offer, and two-business-day acknowledgement commitment.
  3. Practice commitments were implicit. Added a plain-language Urban Strategist commitment covering LGBTQ ownership, disability access, and responsive accommodations.
  4. Case studies named accessibility as a skill but did not show decisions. Added decision, evidence, limitation, and alternative-format notes to selected studies.
  5. Social identity assets were incomplete. Added the canonical Hey Famm glyph to the asset manifest and specimens with an accessible link name.
03 / Known limitations

What still requires care.

  • The Urban Strategist intake form is hosted by Airtable. A direct email route is maintained as the accessible alternative when the embed is difficult to use.
  • Some legacy PDFs, slide decks, and archived standalone pages may require document-specific remediation before public distribution.
  • Complex diagrams require adjacent prose explanations; the diagram alone is not treated as the accessible version.
  • Automated testing cannot establish conformance by itself. Feedback from disabled people takes precedence over a passing tool result.
