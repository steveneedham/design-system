Governance / Accessibility

Access is an acceptance criterion,
not a finishing pass.

Stable components and public artifacts target WCAG 2.2 Level AA. A component is not complete because it looks correct; it must remain understandable and operable across input methods, viewport sizes, motion preferences, and assistive technologies.

01 / Acceptance layer

Eight checks apply before release.

  • StructureUse one clear page title, ordered headings, landmarks, lists, tables, and native controls that communicate their purpose without visual position.
  • Keyboard and focusEvery action works without a pointer. Focus is visible, ordered, unobscured, and never trapped.
  • Contrast and colorText and meaningful UI meet AA contrast. Color is never the only signal for status, ownership, or error.
  • Reflow and zoomContent remains usable at 200% zoom and a 320 CSS-pixel viewport without two-dimensional scrolling except where the information requires it.
  • MotionAnimation is optional, never carries essential meaning, and honors prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Media and graphicsMeaningful images have concise alternatives; complex visuals receive nearby explanations; decorative graphics use empty alternative text.
  • Forms and errorsInputs have persistent labels, instructions, programmatic error association, recoverable validation, and a non-embedded alternative when a third party blocks access.
  • Documents and languagePage language is declared. Downloads use descriptive link text and are tagged, structured, or accompanied by an accessible HTML equivalent.
02 / Definition of done

Evidence travels with the work.

  1. Automated checks have no known critical accessibility errors.
  2. A keyboard-only pass reaches every control in a logical order with visible focus.
  3. Responsive reflow, text zoom, reduced motion, contrast, and non-color meaning are reviewed manually.
  4. Case studies name relevant accessibility decisions, evidence, known limitations, and the accessible alternative.
  5. Exceptions identify an owner, mitigation, and review date. “Third party” is context, not a waiver.
03 / Inclusive language

Describe access without speaking for people.

Use direct, person-respecting language; name specific barriers and remedies; avoid inspiration framing, diagnostic assumptions, and claims of universal access. LGBTQ identity and disability are not decorative credentials. They inform how safety, belonging, privacy, autonomy, and participation are designed into the work.

04 / Feedback and accommodations

A barrier report starts a response.

Accessibility feedback is acknowledged within two business days. Accessible alternatives and reasonable communication accommodations are available on request.
